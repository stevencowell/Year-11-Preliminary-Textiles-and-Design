Section 5.1

Design refinement

Design refinement is the purposeful improvement of a design idea after research, experimentation, feedback and checking. Refinement should respond to evidence rather than happen randomly. A designer considers what has been learned and decides whether a change will better suit the intended user, end use and relevant design criteria.

3 / 8

Section 5.2

Project management

Project management helps organise a textile project so that work can be completed in a realistic and controlled way. A project plan can include milestones, a logical sequence of tasks, estimated time and required resources. Milestones identify important points of progress, while sequencing helps show which tasks need to occur first and which can follow later.

Section 5.3

Evaluation and folio evidence

Evaluation is a supported judgement about how well a textile design meets relevant design criteria. A strong evaluation does more than describe what happened; it uses evidence to explain whether requirements have been met and why. The judgement should remain connected to the intended design criteria rather than relying only on personal opinion.
