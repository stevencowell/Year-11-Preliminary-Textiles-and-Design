Section 10.1

Woven structures

Woven fabrics are formed by interlacing two yarn systems at approximately right angles. Warp yarns run lengthwise through the fabric, while weft yarns pass across the width. The pattern of over-and-under interlacement creates the weave structure. By examining how often yarns cross and how the interlacement repeats, a woven structure can be identified from evidence rather than from surface appearance alone.

Section 10.2

Knitted structures

Knitted fabrics are formed by interlooping yarn rather than interlacing separate warp and weft yarn systems. The horizontal rows of loops are called courses, while the vertical columns are called wales. Because loops can change shape and move in relation to one another, knitted structures commonly provide greater stretch, flexibility and drape than more tightly interlaced structures.

Section 10.3

Non-woven structures

Non-woven textiles are formed by creating a web of fibres and bonding that web without first making yarn. Fibres may be arranged randomly or in a more controlled direction before bonding. Because yarn formation can be bypassed, non-woven production can be relatively efficient, but the resulting properties depend strongly on fibre type, web structure and the method used to hold the fibres together.
