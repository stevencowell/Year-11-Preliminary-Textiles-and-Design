Section 6.1

Fibre classification

A fibre is the smallest textile unit from which yarns and fabrics can be formed. Fibres can first be classified according to their origin. Natural fibres occur in nature and are grouped according to plant, animal or mineral origins. Identifying where a fibre originates helps place it correctly within the broader fibre classification system.

Section 6.2

Molecular and morphological structure

Textile fibres are made from polymers, which are large molecules formed from repeating molecular units joined into chains. Different fibre families contain different kinds of polymers. Cellulose-based fibres contain cellulose polymers, protein-based fibres contain protein polymers, and synthetic fibres are made from manufactured polymers. The chemical structure of these chains contributes to how a fibre behaves.

Section 6.3

From fibre to textile

Textile materials can be understood as a hierarchy from fibre to yarn to fabric to textile product. Fibres are the basic units. Before they become yarn or fabric, fibres may be prepared and aligned so they can be organised into a useful textile structure. Staple fibres are relatively short and generally require spinning to combine them into yarn, while continuous filaments may be used directly or combined to form yar
